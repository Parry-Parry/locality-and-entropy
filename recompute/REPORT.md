## 2. What was wrong

For every unordered pair of miners within each (collection group, loss, architecture) cell, the original code ran an **independent**-samples TOST on per-query scores it had already paired by query id, against a margin defined as **0.1% of the observed difference in means** rather than the pre-stated theta = eps * mu_baseline, at **alpha = 0.10**. It then labelled a pair equivalent when **both one-sided p-values exceeded alpha** -- the exact negation of the TOST decision rule. Because the degenerate margin forces both p-values toward 1, the inverted rule marked 324 of 495 comparisons equivalent; under the correct rule, applied to the very same stored p-values, the count is 0. Full detail, with code quoted, in `AUDIT.md`.

## 7. Caveats

**Statistical power is the dominant issue.** TREC DL 2019 has 43 queries and TREC DL 2020 has 54. From the observed per-query variance, the number of queries needed to demonstrate equivalence is:

| collection | queries available | needed @1% | needed @5% | needed @10% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TREC DL 2019 | 43 | 1136 | 46 | 12 |
| TREC DL 2020 | 54 | 1372 | 56 | 14 |

(Medians over that collection's comparisons, normal approximation at alpha = 0.05.) Demonstrating 1% equivalence on TREC DL would need roughly 26x more queries than exist. **A 1% equivalence claim on TREC DL is not merely unproven, it is unattainable at that sample size** -- which is why the margin matters more than the test. At 10% the requirement (~12 queries) is comfortably met.

**Pooled BEIR.** The single BEIR column of Table 4.1 pools 38238 queries across 24 collections with very different score scales, so its tight intervals overstate precision. It is reported because the original computed it that way and the table shape is fixed; the per-dataset analysis in `results/tost_beir_perdataset.csv` is the primary one.

**Multiple comparisons.** Holm-adjusted TOST p-values are in `p_tost_holm` alongside unadjusted `p_tost`. Adjustment is conservative for equivalence testing and does not change the headline: the comparisons that pass at 1% pass comfortably.

**Transitivity.** Component labels can assert equivalence between two systems whose own pairwise test failed. Retained on instruction; the pairwise truth is in `results/summary.csv`.
